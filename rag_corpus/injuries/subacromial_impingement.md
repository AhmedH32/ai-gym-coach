# ID: COND_SUBACROMIAL_IMPINGEMENT

**Condition Name:** Subacromial Impingement Syndrome

**Category:** Shoulder

**Mechanism of Irritation:** Compression and mechanical attrition of the supraspinatus tendon, long head of biceps tendon, and subacromial bursa within the subacromial space during shoulder abduction and internal rotation.

## Colloquial Symptom Presentation & Provocative Triggers
* **Anatomical Pain Sites:** [front of the shoulder, top of the shoulder, subacromial space, rotator cuff insertion]
* **Sensation Character:** [sharp pinch overhead, deep dull ache at rest, catching sensation, grinding during rotation]
* **Provocative Gym Lifts & Angles:** [heavy overhead barbell press, upright rows above chest, deep chest dips, empty can lateral raises, shoulder abduction past 90 degrees]
* **Gym-Floor Slang Cues:** [_"something is pinching right on top of my shoulder when I press", "it catches every time I lock out an overhead press", "feels like a knife stabbing the front of my shoulder on heavy bench"_]

## Absolute Contraindications (Exercises to Avoid)

* Overhead pressing with internal rotation (e.g., Behind-the-neck press)
* Upright rows above chest height
* Dips extending into extreme shoulder extension
* Empty can exercise (abduction in internal rotation)

## Biomechanical Modifications (Rules)

* Limit shoulder abduction to below 90 degrees or press in the scapular plane (30-45 degrees anterior to coronal plane)
* Maintain external rotation during pressing movements (e.g., neutral grip / palms facing each other)
* Avoid full end-range internal rotation during loaded overhead movements

## Recommended Substitutions

* Landmine overhead press instead of standing barbell overhead press
* Neutral-grip dumbbell shoulder press instead of wide-grip barbell shoulder press
* High-to-low cable face pulls emphasizing external rotation
