# ID: COND_HAMSTRING_STRAIN

**Condition Name:** Hamstring Muscle Strain

**Category:** Hip

**Mechanism of Irritation:** Acute tear or micro-damage of the hamstring muscle complex (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus) during rapid eccentric lengthening at the hip or knee during terminal swing or heavy hip hinging.

## Colloquial Symptom Presentation & Provocative Triggers
* **Anatomical Pain Sites:** High hammy near the sit bone, middle muscle belly, back of the thigh, crease under the glute.
* **Sensation Character:** Sudden sharp pop, deep tearing sensation, hot pulling ache, tight cramping grip.
* **Provocative Gym Lifts & Angles:** Heavy Romanian deadlifts at bottom stretch, maximum deficit stiff-leg deadlifts, sprint top-end stride extension, deep kettlebell swings.
* **Gym-Floor Slang Cues:** "My hammy grabbed me coming out of the hole", "feels like a pulled string in my leg", "tweak in the back of my thigh on that last pull".

## Absolute Contraindications (Exercises to Avoid)

* Heavy Jefferson curls or maximum end-range hamstring stretching under load
* Explosive sprinting or high-velocity sprinting strides during recovery phase
* Maximum weight straight-leg deadlifts with rounded lower back

## Biomechanical Modifications (Rules)

* Limit hip flexion angle when knee is fully extended during early rehabilitation
* Utilize progressive submaximal eccentric loading (e.g., Nordic hamstring curls) at controlled speeds
* Keep knee slightly bent during hip hinging movements to decrease distal tendon strain

## Recommended Substitutions

* Glute bridge with feet close to hips instead of deep Romanian deadlifts
* Swiss ball hamstring curls with controlled range of motion
* Isometric single-leg bridge holds
