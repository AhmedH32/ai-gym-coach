# ID: COND_PLANTAR_FASCIITIS

**Condition Name:** Plantar Fasciitis

**Category:** Ankle

**Mechanism of Irritation:** Micro-tearing and degenerative inflammation of the plantar fascia aponeurosis at its calcaneal insertion, provoked by high impact, excessive foot pronation, or sudden dorsiflexion load.

## Colloquial Symptom Presentation & Provocative Triggers
* **Anatomical Pain Sites:** bottom of the heel, heel bone insertion, medial calcaneal tubercle, arch of the foot
* **Sensation Character:** sharp stabbing pain, first-step morning stiffness, burning ache under load, pulling tightness in the arch
* **Provocative Gym Lifts & Angles:** barefoot heavy calf raises with deep heel drops, high-impact jump rope, running on hard treadmills, plyometric box jumps with forefoot landing
* **Gym-Floor Slang Cues:** "feels like a nail driving straight into my heel when I step out of bed", "burning right under my heel bone every time I bounce on my toes", "my arch feels super tight and pulls whenever I go barefoot"

## Absolute Contraindications (Exercises to Avoid)

* High-impact barefoot jumping or plyometrics
* Barefoot heavy calf raises into deep negative heel drop
* High-volume unconditioned running on hard concrete surfaces

## Biomechanical Modifications (Rules)

* Wear supportive footwear with adequate arch support during all standing resistance training
* Avoid deep negative heel drops past neutral during calf raises in acute phases
* Utilize controlled eccentric calf loading with a towel under toes (windlass mechanism loading)

## Recommended Substitutions

* Seated calf raises limited to neutral heel depth instead of standing deep-drop calf raises
* Non-weight-bearing cardiorespiratory training (e.g., rowing or stationary bike) instead of running
* Controlled isometric calf holds at neutral foot angle
