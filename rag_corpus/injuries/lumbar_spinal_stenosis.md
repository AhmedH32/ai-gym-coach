# ID: COND_LUMBAR_SPINAL_STENOSIS

**Condition Name:** Lumbar Spinal Stenosis

**Category:** Back

**Mechanism of Irritation:** Narrowing of the central spinal canal or neural foramina, leading to vascular or mechanical compression of spinal nerves, typically exacerbated by extension and relieved by slight spinal flexion.

## Colloquial Symptom Presentation & Provocative Triggers
* **Anatomical Pain Sites:** Lower back, belt line, buttocks, back of thighs, gluteal crease
* **Sensation Character:** Dull heavy ache, bilateral leg numbness, tingling, burning heaviness, cramping tightness
* **Provocative Gym Lifts & Angles:** Standing overhead barbell press, heavy standing back squats with high arch, prone back extensions, standing upright walking on treadmill, flat bench press with aggressive arch
* **Gym-Floor Slang Cues:** "My legs turn to cement when standing around," "My lower back jammed up when standing tall," "My glutes get numb unless I lean over the dumbbells"

## Absolute Contraindications (Exercises to Avoid)

* Prolonged standing spinal extension
* Heavy axial loading in extension (e.g., back squat with pronounced lordosis)
* Prone back extensions

## Biomechanical Modifications (Rules)

* Adopt a slight flexion-bias during hip hinging and lower body movements
* Perform seated or incline cycling exercises instead of standing upright extension movements
* Maintain abdominal hollow/posterior pelvic tilt during exercises

## Recommended Substitutions

* Incline leg press with controlled depth instead of heavy standing back squat
* Seated cable rowing instead of standing bent-over row
* Cat-cow (flexion emphasis) and curl-ups for core endurance
