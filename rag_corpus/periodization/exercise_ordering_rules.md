# ID: PROG_EXERCISE_ORDERING
**Topic:** Exercise Sequencing, CNS Demand, and Fatigue Hierarchies
**Category:** Biomechanics
**Evidence Base:** Simão R et al. (2012, PMID: 22027847); NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning (4th Ed.).

## Tiered Exercise Hierarchy
1. **Tier 1: Free-Weight Multi-Joint Compounds (Primary CNS Load):** Barbell Back Squat, Conventional/Sumo Deadlift, Flat/Incline Barbell Bench Press, Standing Overhead Press. Program first in the session when the central nervous system (CNS) and stabilizing musculature are un-fatigued.
2. **Tier 2: Machine/Supported Multi-Joint Compounds (Secondary Tension):** Incline Dumbbell Press, Leg Press, Hack Squat, Chest-Supported T-Bar Row, Romanian Deadlift. Delivers high mechanical tension with reduced spinal axial compression.
3. **Tier 3: Single-Joint Targeted Isolation & Cables:** Pec Deck Fly, Cable Lateral Raise, Leg Extension, Seated Leg Curl, Triceps Rope Pushdown. Maximizes localized metabolic stress and end-range mechanical tension with minimal systemic fatigue.
4. **Tier 4: Postural, Corrective & Scapular Stabilizers:** Cable Face Pulls, Push-Up Plus, Prone Y-Raises, External Rotations, Hanging Knee Raises. Program at session end.

## Biomechanical Sequencing Constraints
- Never schedule heavy axial loading movements (e.g., Barbell Back Squats and Conventional Deadlifts) on consecutive days.
- If a specific muscle group is identified as a priority or weak point, place its primary exercise at the absolute beginning of the session after a general warm-up.
